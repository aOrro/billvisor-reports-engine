# Phase 1 Recommendation Framework

## Status and ownership

- **Status:** Partially approved; decision mapping and thresholds remain unresolved.
- **Owner:** CEO, subject to founder approval.
- **Purpose:** Convert an analysed service into one clear household action without treating the Bills Health Score as an automatic recommendation.
- **Applies to:** Broadband, mobile and combined or separate energy analysis in Phase 1.
- **Does not replace:** Service-specific evidence methods, the Bills Health Score method, provider-quality methods or founder approval of live-case recommendations.

The framework is founder-defined Phase 1 policy to validate. It is not yet supported by live customer-outcome data.

## 1. Approved recommendation types

Every analysed service receives one specific customer action falling within one of these internal types:

1. **Stay put** — the current service is sufficiently competitive and action is unlikely to justify the household's effort.
2. **Improve the current setup** — a change that does not require switching may improve the outcome, such as changing payment method, renegotiating or moving to a better-fit plan.
3. **Look at changing now** — current evidence indicates a material opportunity and acting now appears practical.
4. **Review later** — a possible opportunity exists, but acting at a known later point is more sensible.
5. **Confirm information** — an agreed minimum customer input is missing or a disclosed specialist arrangement changes what minimum evidence is required.

These are internal action types, not mandatory customer-facing labels. Customer wording must name the concrete action in plain English.

## 2. Approved input boundary

The eventual recommendation considers:

- whether the agreed minimum customer information was provided;
- potential financial value, using both the annual amount and its share of current spending;
- the current service position, including the service score where supportable;
- expected household effort and disruption; and
- timing and known constraints, including contract end date and exit charges where known.

The service score supports but does not determine the recommendation. A Poor, Medium or Good band must not map automatically to a recommendation type.

## 3. Customer-information sufficiency

When the customer provides the agreed minimum information for an ordinary supported case, Billvisor treats the customer submission as sufficient. Billvisor must not request deliberately unrequired information or describe the customer's submission as insufficient.

An internal market-evidence or methodology gap belongs to Billvisor, not the customer. Billvisor must still provide the most useful recommendation that the available evidence can support. Where the gap materially restricts the assessment, use **Analysed with limitations** and explain the one material limitation without blaming the customer.

Use **Confirm information** only when:

- an agreed minimum customer field is missing; or
- the customer discloses a specialist arrangement for which the ordinary minimum is not sufficient under the approved service method.

## 4. Contract status

Contract status does not currently change the Bills Health Score. It may change the timing, practicality and urgency of the recommended action.

Being out of contract does not by itself prove that a better household outcome is available. Price comparison and provider-quality evidence continue to establish the service position. If a worthwhile opportunity exists, being out of contract may make acting now more practical.

## 5. Approved decision sequence

Apply the following sequence to each requested service:

1. **Check the agreed minimum information.** If an agreed minimum field is missing, use **Confirm information**. If it is present, continue; do not turn an internal evidence gap into a customer-information failure.
2. **Identify the supported opportunities.** Consider a supported improvement to the current setup, a supported change of provider or plan, or neither. Do not invent an opportunity merely to avoid a stay-put result.
3. **Decide whether an opportunity is worth the household's effort.** Consider financial value, service position and likely effort or disruption together. If nothing appears worthwhile, use **Stay put**.
4. **Check whether acting now is practical.** If the worthwhile action is practical now, use **Improve the current setup** or **Look at changing now** as applicable. If a known timing constraint makes later action more sensible, use **Review later**.
5. **Choose one action.** Where several actions are supportable, choose the strongest overall household outcome. Do not automatically choose the largest saving or the easiest action.

This sequence does not establish automatic numeric recommendation thresholds. Those require realistic-case calibration and founder approval.

## 6. Approved effort levels

Classify the effort of the specific proposed action, not the service category in general:

- **Low effort:** One straightforward change, normally with the current provider, with no known equipment, installation or service-transfer risk.
- **Moderate effort:** Comparison and a standard plan or provider change, with no known unusual complication.
- **High effort:** A known installation requirement, equipment handling, bundle dependency, meaningful disruption risk or other case-specific complication.

Do not present these levels as measured facts or attach rigid time estimates until pilot evidence supports them.

Apply the levels qualitatively:

- a smaller supported benefit may justify a low-effort action;
- a moderate-effort action requires a clearer supported benefit; and
- a high-effort action requires a strong supported benefit, otherwise prefer **Review later** or **Stay put** as the evidence supports.

No monetary threshold or automatic recommendation mapping is approved by this section.

## 7. Approved calibration cases

These cases calibrate founder judgement; they do not create automatic thresholds unless a later decision explicitly does so.

### Case 1 — Material broadband opportunity, out of contract

- Current price: £39 per month.
- Similar-service comparison: £23.85 per month.
- Potential annual saving: approximately £182, about 39% of current annual spending.
- Service score: 39 — Poor.
- Contract status: out of contract.
- Proposed action effort: Moderate.
- **Approved recommendation type:** Look at changing now.
- **Reason:** The supported opportunity is substantial in both cash and percentage terms, and no known contract-timing reason requires delay. This recommends examining a change now, not switching blindly.

## 8. Unresolved work

The following remain subject to founder approval:

- realistic-case calibration of financial value against the approved effort levels;
- when contract timing changes **Look at changing now** to **Review later**;
- the minimum support required for a recommendation under an internal evidence limitation;
- whether any parts can become deterministic after pilot validation.
