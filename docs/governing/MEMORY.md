# Business Memory

## Purpose
Build a trusted, independent advocate for households: turn bills and contracts into clear, explainable recommendations that reduce effort and increase confidence. The outcome may be to switch, stay put, or gather more information.

## Launch decision
- **Launch market: UK first.** This replaces the previous Spain-only decision.
- Default to UK households, providers, consumer expectations, regulation, data/privacy requirements and distribution channels. Do not import Spanish assumptions.
- Research the alternatives households actually use and do not assume differentiation without direct evidence.

## Phase 1
- Validate demand and trust before product automation: a simple landing page with email capture, followed by a conversational email service that requests the information and documents needed to produce a personalised Bills Health Report.
- Reports may be produced manually behind the scenes. Learn before scaling.
- Reports are free during Phase 1. Their aim is clarity: tell users what is worth their time to look at and what is not.
- Reports provide analysis, one clear next step and a brief explanation of why. They do not initially provide specific deals.
- Keep the welcome-email evidence request deliberately light. Broadband: provider, speed, monthly cost and end date. Mobile: provider, data allowance, important extras or handset inclusion, monthly cost and end date. Gas/electricity: postcode, supplier, whether gas, electricity or both, approximate monthly payment, how the bill is paid, tariff type/name and end date if known, plus number of bedrooms when annual usage is unavailable. Invite a recent bill because it can improve accuracy, but do not require one. Ask the user to mention prepayment or multi-rate metering, electric heating, a heat pump or regular home EV charging because the ordinary bedroom-based fallback may not apply. Users may reply with what they know or attach a relevant document. Review supplied information first and ask one consolidated follow-up only for decision-critical gaps; never assume one document contains everything.
- The approved welcome-email copy is [PHASE_1_WELCOME_EMAIL_TEMPLATE.md](../operations/PHASE_1_WELCOME_EMAIL_TEMPLATE.md). It asks for the postcode and the requested service details in one reply, using a scannable fill-in layout; it does not ask for the postcode alone first.
- For an energy price calculation, prefer the household's actual previous-12-month consumption or the supplier's estimated annual consumption for each fuel. When neither is available, the ordinary fallback uses the current Ofgem Typical Domestic Consumption Values selected by the comparison-industry bedroom convention: 1–2 bedrooms = low (1,600 kWh electricity; 6,000 kWh gas), 3–4 bedrooms = medium (2,500 kWh electricity; 9,500 kWh gas), and 5+ bedrooms = high (3,800 kWh electricity; 14,000 kWh gas). These values took effect on 1 July 2026. Bedrooms alone select the profile; do not add property type, occupant count or other variables. This bedroom mapping is an adopted comparison-industry convention, not an Ofgem-defined household classification. Monthly payment is a sense-check, not a substitute for consumption. Customer communications must explain only that consumption was estimated from households with similar characteristics; keep the source, bands and arithmetic internal. When the agreed minimum information is supplied, retain the coverage status **Analysed** while clearly describing the resulting energy figures as estimates. Do not apply the ordinary bedroom-based estimate to specialist or multi-rate arrangements, including materially different usage patterns such as electric heating, heat pumps or home EV charging, when the required usage evidence is unavailable.
- Keep the Phase 1 energy output narrow. Perform three default checks only: whether a lower-cost payment method appears worthwhile; whether the current fixed/variable tariff and any known end date or exit fee make review timely; and whether accessible current tariff evidence indicates that looking elsewhere is worth the household's time. The additional customer-facing energy narrative contains the estimated annual cost, a potential saving only where supportable, a short explanation and one recommended action. Supportable energy Bills Health Scores and the existing materially triggered provider-quality note remain part of the approved report; this narrow-output rule does not remove them. Mention standing-charge effects only when they materially affect a low-usage household; do not add routine generic energy-saving tips or extensive tariff education. Possible actions include stay put, check a lower-cost payment method, look more closely at current tariffs, or confirm annual usage when the estimate is too uncertain. Do not imply whole-market coverage.
- V1 supports broadband, mobile, gas and electricity. Water is out of scope: household customers generally cannot choose their water provider, so it does not currently offer enough actionable review value for Phase 1. Users may submit any combination of the supported services. Every report assigns each service one coverage status: **Analysed**, **Analysed with limitations** or **Not analysed**. Analysed and Analysed-with-limitations services receive a service page; Not-analysed services appear only in the overview. When gas and electricity share one combined analysis, the overview shows one **Energy** row rather than duplicating gas and electricity. These are coverage controls, not market-position ratings or recommendations. Use Analysed with limitations when the user requested the review but missing agreed minimum information or an internal evidence gap restricted the assessment; deliberately unrequested information is not a limitation.

Northern Ireland energy is deferred until a separate market methodology, evidence base and customer need are validated. This does not automatically exclude Northern Ireland households from broadband or mobile support.

- The partially approved Phase 1 recommendation framework uses five internal action types: **Stay put**, **Improve the current setup**, **Look at changing now**, **Review later** and **Confirm information**. These are internal categories; customer wording names the concrete action. The service score informs but never automatically determines the action. If the customer supplies the agreed minimum information for an ordinary supported case, treat the customer submission as sufficient; any material market-evidence or methodology gap is Billvisor's limitation and may produce **Analysed with limitations**, not a claim that the customer failed to provide enough. Use **Confirm information** only when an agreed minimum field is missing or a disclosed specialist arrangement changes the required minimum. Contract status affects timing, practicality and urgency but does not currently change the score. For each service, check minimum information, identify supported current-setup or provider/plan-change opportunities, judge value together with effort and service position, apply known timing constraints, then choose one strongest household action rather than automatically selecting the largest saving or easiest action. Classify the proposed action itself qualitatively as Low, Moderate or High effort; the required strength of benefit rises with the effort. These are not measured time promises. Numeric value-versus-effort and timing rules remain unresolved and founder-owned. Use [PHASE_1_RECOMMENDATION_FRAMEWORK.md](PHASE_1_RECOMMENDATION_FRAMEWORK.md) as the current authority.
- While the recommendation framework remains partially approved, use the founder-approved single-owner case-production route in [PHASE_1_CASE_OPERATING_METHOD.md](../operations/PHASE_1_CASE_OPERATING_METHOD.md). Operations owns an ordinary live case from intake through delivery and applies approved deterministic calculations directly. The founder performs only the interactive searches requested by Operations, supplies the captures, approves the exact recommendation and customer content, checks the generated HTML visually and manually saves it as PDF. Evidence is exception-only for a new provider assessment, a missing or ambiguous source/method rule, a material conflict or a case the calculator cannot safely process. Review is risk-based rather than mandatory on every report: it checks the first five live reports, calculator failures/manual overrides/new or ambiguous methods, every primary **Look at changing now** recommendation while the framework remains unvalidated, and then one in five otherwise ordinary reports. A Critical or Major defect resets the clean-run count until five consecutive reviewed reports pass. Operations initiates any Review handoff; the founder does not coordinate it. Non-sampled ordinary cases use the deterministic validation gate, while founder content approval remains mandatory in every case. There is no automated/headless PDF renderer or post-export Review step.
- Evidence's ordinary live-case broadband/mobile offer task is deliberately narrow: use the founder-pasted results for broadly comparable packages, calculate each package's approximate effective monthly price across the first 12 months where promotional periods or stated rises make that necessary, include compulsory charges and exclude rewards. Group the usable packages by provider and calculate the mean effective monthly price for each provider. The live-case benchmark is the median of those provider-level means, preventing providers with more observed listings from dominating the result. Where a possible future rise is unquantified, use the advertised price as a reasonable working estimate rather than turning the case into deeper research. Exclude only results with no usable price or that are plainly not comparable. Evidence returns the benchmark and the smallest reproducible internal trace; it does not make the household recommendation or claim whole-market coverage. There is no fixed time cap; speed should come from this narrow scope. Do not apply this broadband/mobile offer-list method to energy, which remains governed by its household-specific tariff method.
- In the Phase 1 customer report, current annual spend for broadband and mobile is the submitted monthly payment multiplied by 12. For Energy, use the current tariff's calculated annual cost from the approved usage/rate method; the customer-supplied approximate monthly payment is a sense-check only because it may reflect credit, debit or budget smoothing. The internal annual comparison benchmark is the median provider mean multiplied by 12. Where the founder judges that the positive difference is useful, the report may show an estimated potential annual saving calculated as current annual spend minus the annual benchmark. Never show a £0 potential saving. Retain exact values internally and round all customer-facing annual figures to the nearest whole pound. Do not expose the internal word “benchmark”: describe the comparison as what similar services cost, using “about” for that approximate comparison figure. Do not prefix the potential-saving figure with “about” because the adjacent wording already identifies it as an estimate. Current annual spend remains explicitly annualised from the submitted monthly payment rather than described as a forecast. Keep provider-level averages, individual offers, source lists, sample arithmetic and the research process internal. The customer sees current annual spending, what similar services cost, an estimated positive saving where useful, a short conclusion and one next action. The approved template may also show the household’s own submitted current-service details where needed to explain the analysis, such as provider/supplier, plan, speed or allowance, payment, price and contract/tariff timing. Those details are not comparison evidence: they must appear in the founder-approval package and be rendered only from the approved report data. The approved visual system is reusable and conditional: page count changes with the included service pages. Green outcomes should positively confirm that staying put is sensible. Green-versus-amber classification and what counts as a useful saving remain founder case judgements until numeric thresholds are separately approved; do not automate or invent them.
- The founder-approved report design includes overall and service-level Bills Health Scores. The authority is [BILLS_HEALTH_SCORE_METHOD.md](BILLS_HEALTH_SCORE_METHOD.md). Service score remains 90% price position and 10% tie-safe provider position, with price curve `min(100, 20 + 60 × comparison/current)`. After the 90/10 calculation, cap at 39 when potential annual saving is at least £150 and 25% of current annual spend; cap at 74 when it is at least £100 and 15%; otherwise do not apply a materiality cap. Where the founder-approved **Stay put** recommendation is specifically because a supported saving below £50 is not worth the effort, apply the explicit `small_saving_not_worth_effort` score floor of 75; still display the saving. A reasonable incomplete broadband/mobile comparison may produce a numeric score under Analysed with limitations, with its qualified-comparison status retained internally. Do not label the customer-facing score or gauge **Estimated**; explain the relevant estimate or limitation in the service-page narrative instead. Energy may receive a price-only tariff-fit score when the current tariff is compared with a relevant accessible tariff using the same selected household-consumption input, geography, fuels, meter type and payment basis. It does not establish a best deal, whole-market position or alternative-provider saving, and provider quality is not included. With the approved bedroom fallback, explain the estimated usage in the customer narrative but keep coverage as **Analysed** where the agreed minimum data was supplied. The overall score is the annual-spend and status-weighted average of numeric service scores: current annual spend × 1.0 for Analysed, or × 0.8 for Analysed with limitations. Services without a numeric score and Not-analysed services are excluded. Poor remains 0–39, Medium 40–74 and Good 75–100. The rules are founder-defined Phase 1 policy, calibrated only on limited controlled/live cases; real-world comprehension and calibration remain untested.
- The authoritative HTML-rendering rules are in [PHASE_1_REPORT_RENDERER_CONTRACT.md](../operations/PHASE_1_REPORT_RENDERER_CONTRACT.md). In short: render approved report data only; remove report-confidence and time/difficulty claims; use founder-selected Low/Moderate/High effort; map Market position directly from a numeric score band (or `—`); place limitations/provider note/energy usage explanation in **What this means**; show Energy monthly payment alongside calculated annual tariff cost; and show the source-details line once on the final action-plan page.
- The report overview retains Service, Annual spend, Market position, Potential savings and Health score; it does not show a next-action column. Do not show automatic High/Medium priority chips: use action order, with **No action needed** for Stay put. Normalise a clearly stated Energy monthly payment for display only; omit it if ambiguous. Energy's fixed explanation uses the annual energy-use figure available for the home, so it remains true for actual or estimated usage. Display a positive founder-approved Energy saving beneath the annual cost. Broadband/Mobile comparison figures say **Similar services cost about £X a year**. The report uses the renderer contract's approved code/date display wording.
- The provider-quality note is a material explanation, not a routine provider description. Calculate and band the price-only score, then calculate and band the combined 90/10 service score. Show a negative provider-quality note only when the combined service score falls into a lower Poor/Medium/Good band than the price-only result. Use the short template: **“Recent public data indicates that your current provider has weaker [service-relevant quality areas] results than some others. This lowered your [service] rating from [price-only band] to [combined band].”** Use **reliability and customer support** for broadband, **network experience and customer support** for mobile, and **billing and customer support** for energy. Use “public data” only when the underlying sources are genuinely publicly accessible. Do not show a positive provider note when provider position is strong, and show no provider note when provider quality does not change the service band. Do not expose source names, internal table positions, ranks or scores, call the evidence “reviews,” label a provider simply good or bad, or imply whole-market certainty. Include one report-wide line: **“Supporting source details are available on request.”** Retain exact sources, dates, covered periods, limitations and calculations internally so that request can be fulfilled.
- Energy uses two provider-scoring categories: Billing & Metering 50% and Customer Support & Complaint Resolution 50%. Account & Switching Administration is not separately scored because comparable provider-level evidence was not found; relevant final-bill or complaint evidence maps to the applicable category, while other switching/account incidents remain context or caution evidence without invented points. The equal split is a founder-approved Phase 1 simplification, not a data-proven optimum. Serious regulatory or financial concerns sit outside the numeric score as a caution overlay. The approved hierarchy prioritises Citizens Advice underlying supplier data, Ofgem and Energy Ombudsman evidence, with explicit anti-double-counting rules. Initial research covers Octopus Energy, British Gas, E.ON Next, OVO Energy, EDF Energy and ScottishPower, then adds any supplier appearing in a live customer's current service or credible alternatives. Group evidence must not automatically be transferred to a separately operated retail brand. Numeric normalisation thresholds remain pending; do not score providers before they are approved.
- Success is trust, useful reports, repeat uploads and referrals—not revenue or switching volume.

## Principles
- Represent households, never providers or our own commercial interests.
- Optimise for the overall household outcome, not the lowest price or a transaction.
- Make every recommendation evidence-based, clearly explained and easy to act on.
- Ask only for information that materially improves the result; reduce cognitive load.
- Validate assumptions with real evidence and behaviour before building.
- Trust wins over revenue, growth and automation.

## Positioning
- Not a comparison site, switching service, budgeting app, bank, financial adviser or marketplace.
- Long term: a proactive, trusted household bills/recurring expenses advisor and collective advocate.
- Proposed positioning hypothesis to validate: independent, explainable advice where staying put can be the correct recommendation; never switch-first or commission-led.

## Operating rule
**Before making an assumption, ask the founder.** Clearly distinguish facts, confirmed decisions and open questions.

**Do not aim to please, validate or reassure the founder.** Provide objective, evidence-backed information; challenge unsupported claims constructively. Explicitly state when a conclusion, recommendation or statement is not fully supported by data, and never present it as fact.
